# CBT Chaos Team Learnings

Reusable, evidence-backed guidance for the agent-team chaos orchestration.
Follows the same maintenance rules as cbt-chaos-run-learnings.md: dated
entries only when a closed run establishes a generalizable lesson; every
entry cites run artifacts (repository-relative, sanitized); dedupe before
adding; never include secrets or private hostnames; skill changes require
user confirmation.

## Learnings

- **2026-10-16 — accept the armed observer only after independent verification, and freeze the pack only after every writer stops (CBT-F-01 r04 / CBT-I-01 r01):** *confirmed observation* — the ordering that makes a team run auditable is mechanical, not advisory: the observer's four watches must be armed **before** the trigger (both runs' baselines were empty at ARMED, proving arming preceded creation), the Lead must re-verify the recorded helper/oc PIDs itself (`kill -0`), and the evidence index can only be regenerated after the last writer stops. *Expected vs actual:* the F-01 freeze attempt stalled because the observer's `FINAL` arrived after the run was terminal (its own poll-loop bug delayed `PRESERVED` by 6 m 22 s); in I-01 the same handoff landed 14 s after the request, and its watches were stopped before the freeze. *Reproduction status:* `confirmed` (both directions observed). *Limitation:* the pack the checkers graded must be cited by its own hash when a post-close regeneration happens (I-01: graded `dfaa18e1…`, regenerated after the two post-close notes). *Next:* read `ARMED` and the watch PIDs, not the observer's prose; and treat "index is the last write" as a gate (`pack-validate.sh`). Evidence: `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-F-01/runs/20261016-cbt-f-01-r04/{observer/{ARMED,PRESERVED,FINAL,finding.md},ledger.jsonl}`, `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-I-01/runs/20261016-cbt-i-01-r01/{observer/** ,ledger.jsonl}`.
- **2026-10-16 — never pull a run root over Lead-owned state, and never hand-author what a template already says (CBT-F-01 r04 / CBT-I-01 r01; findings HV2/HV3):** *confirmed observation* — two classes cost real work: (a) `make pull-reports` plus a targeted rsync restored `ledger.jsonl`/`state.json` from the execution host and silently reverted the Lead's post-push events because those files existed only on the workstation (the trail had to be re-appended and marked `ledger_note`); (b) a hand-written per-run observer contract dropped the checkpoint-tree capture that `cbt-chaos/contracts/observer.md` already mandates, and the I-01 hand-written checker contracts pointed at the wrong spec section (§4 instead of §5 — the templates say §5). *Expected vs actual:* the run root is described as living on the execution host and being pulled; in practice the Lead is its sole writer for those files, so "pull everything" is the wrong default. *Reproduction status:* `confirmed` (both runs). *Next:* exclude `ledger.jsonl`/`state.json`/`evidence-index.json` when pulling, or append the ledger on the authoritative side; and generate per-run contracts with `cbt-chaos/contract-gen.sh` instead of authoring them. Evidence: `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-F-01/runs/20261016-cbt-f-01-r04/ledger.jsonl` (`ledger_note`), `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-I-01/runs/20261016-cbt-i-01-r01/ledger.jsonl` (`contract_correction_note`, `index_regenerated`), `cbt-chaos/contracts/observer.md:63`.
- **2026-10-16 — the twice-rule is what turns a run defect into a mandatory edit, and those edits are human-gated (both runs, learning closeout):** *confirmed observation* — three failure classes reached ≥2 occurrences across these runs: evaluator-out-of-shape (HV1), a per-run contract dropping a template mandate (HV3), and `deletionTimestamp` read as a deletion instant (resolved as watch semantics: it is the deletion *deadline*, and `deletionGracePeriodSeconds` moves 60 → 0 exactly where it regresses). Per the team contract the Scenario Improver must then produce the exact diff and the human must approve or explicitly decline it; nothing was applied. *Expected vs actual:* the escalation worked as designed — the improver produced diffs (evaluator shape switch, trigger artifact writer, contract generation, a `deletionTimestamp` reading rule) and the runs' ledgers record the approval as outstanding. *Reproduction status:* `confirmed`. *Limitation:* the improver's proposals are large (the I-01 set is ~1400 lines) and need a human decision loop, not an agent. *Next:* the human approves or declines each class in the ledger, then the approved diffs land as one reviewed change. Evidence: `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-F-01/analysis/20261016-cbt-f-01-r04-improvements.md`, `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-I-01/analysis/20261016-cbt-i-01-r01-improvements.md`, both runs' `ledger.jsonl` (`learning_closeout`).
- **2026-10-16 — design the pre-state witness against the injection's own timing, or the whole verdict blocks (CBT-EXP-06 r02):** *confirmed observation* — r02's observer contract required it to prove the target per-disk checkpoint bitmap present before the disruptor removed it, but its pre-capture loop sampled every 10 s, resolved the checkpoint name only at 04:28:19Z, lost its three in-window samples to the launcher swap (`exec_failed` 04:28:23/33/44Z), and therefore recorded **0 of 48 rows with `target_present=true`**. Both blind checkers independently rejected on that single gap (evidence C2 BLOCKED, injection REJECT), which by policy forces BLOCKED even though the post-state, the per-disk contrast, the redefined checkpoint XML (only `vda`) and the product's own `virError(Code=109)` detection were each independently captured. *Expected vs actual:* the injection was deterministic and fail-closed (the trigger refuses to act unless the bitmap is present, and QEMU's empty success reply to `block-dirty-bitmap-remove` cannot come from a missing name), yet that read is injector output and the protocol forbids it as sole support — so the outcome could not be attributed. *Reproduction status:* `confirmed` (1 run, 2 independent checkers, upstream re-graded once and upheld). *Limitation:* the rejection is provenance-only; it says nothing about whether the surgery worked. *Next:* before arming, pre-resolve every identity the witness needs (checkpoint name, QEMU node, target UID) from the product's own records; capture pre-state immediately before and post-state immediately after the mutation with no cadence gap (event-driven or ≤1 s), and keep the disruptor's own read-back as corroboration only. Same class, other direction: the Lead's scaffold push also clobbered Lead-owned state (an empty local `ledger.jsonl` overwrote six remote events and they had to be re-appended as reconstructions), extending that rule from pulls to pushes. Evidence: `cbt-chaos/scenarios_v2/CBT-EXP-06/runs/20261016-exp06-r02/{observer/ARMED,observer/effect/pre-bitmap-history.jsonl,checkers/injection-validity.json,checkers/evidence-sufficiency.json,ledger.jsonl}`, `.../contracts/observer.md`, `.../recovery/recovery-report.json`.
